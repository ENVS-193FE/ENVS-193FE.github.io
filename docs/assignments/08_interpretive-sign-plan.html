Overview

Your second class project is an interpretive trail sign. This can be an expansion on your field guide, or you can use this opportunity to learn about a new ecosystem or site.

You do not have to make your sign for a class field site. The only requirement is that your site is situated in California.

Prior to completing this planning assignment:

  • read the final directions for the interpretive sign
  • review the examples linked in the directions
  • fill out this google sheet with your name, the site you have chosen, and the dominant ecosystem type.
Sign up for a site and ecosystem soon!

You may not focus on the same site as a classmate. If you feel compelled by a particular location, then sign up for it!

Assignment

Basic information

In 1-3 sentences each, outline your ideas for the following:

  • the site on which you want to focus
  • the dominant ecosystem at that site
  • why you are interested in this site or ecosystem
  • historical human interactions with the site or ecosystem
  • current human interactions with the site or ecosystem

Species of interest

For each of these components, describe why they are relevant to the site and ecosystem. Note that you may write more than 3 sentences for these components if needed.

  • 2-3 primary producers
  • 2-3 consumers
  • 2-3 predators

Visual representations

Note that you may write more than 3 sentences for these components if needed.

Describe how you plan to visually represent:

  • the “wide view” of the ecosystem (examples include but are not limited to: map, landscape, cross-section), and why
  • species of interest and their interactions with each other and the environment

Outline

Lastly, draw a potential layout for your interpretive sign. Use stick figures or coarse sketches for visual components, and shapes representing placeholders for each written section (including the title).
