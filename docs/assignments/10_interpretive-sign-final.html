Overview and goals

What is an interpretive sign?

Interpretive signs are often placed along trails to educate people passing by about what they are observing, and often communicate why it is important to study or protect an area or species. For example, Campus Lagoon has many interpretive signs explaining the diﬀerent habitats in the area and species that CCBER works to restore. These signs serve to provide information to the public about what work is currently happening at the lagoon and why it is important. For example, visitors reading a sign about bioswales will know why CCBER employees are working to construct these wetlands around Manzanita Village.

Goals

Your final interpretive sign should communicate two main messages to the audience (visitors to the field site): 1) why these species are worth conserving/studying and, and 2) why this habitat is worth conserving/studying.

To communicate these messages to the audience, you should include, at minimum:

  • a wide view of the ecosystem: what is the landscape or dominant ecosystem types?
  • plant species that occur at that ecosystem: what plants are common in this ecosystem type? How do they characterize the ecosystem?
  • faunal species that occur at that ecosystem at different trophic levels: what species occur at the site, and how do they interact (with each other and with the environment)?

You will also demonstrate your ability to distill sources of information for a broad audience. At this point in the quarter, you have experience reading many different kinds of texts: original studies, reviews, field guides, etc. In this assignment, you will integrate information from a variety of sources to tell a story about an ecosystem.
